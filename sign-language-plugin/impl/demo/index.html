CONTENT

Why non-manual markers are not decoration

Sign languages are natural languages with their own grammar. American Sign Language is not a manual code for English: it has its own word order, its own morphology, and it uses the space in front of the signer to mark reference and agreement.

A large part of that grammar lives on the face. Raised eyebrows turn a clause into a yes-or-no question. Lowered eyebrows mark a wh-question. A headshake carries negation across the whole phrase it accompanies. An avatar that moves only its hands is not signing with an accent — it is producing sentences whose grammatical marking has been deleted.

That is why this rig spends 16 morph targets on brows, eyes and mouth, and why the compiler treats them as part of the sign rather than as polish added afterwards. Click this paragraph and watch the face, not just the hands.

INTEGRATION

What a host page has to add

<script defer src="/sla-plugin.js"></script>
<script>
  window.onload = () => {
    window.sla = new SignPlugin({
      token: 'demo_2f6a91c4e7b3',
      language: 'ase',
      avatar: 'NOOR'
    });
  };
</script>

Everything else — colours, side, capture mode, exception selectors — comes from /v1/config/<token>.json, so a customer changes their setup without touching their own code.

Avatar, rig, animation data, wire format, backend and plugin are all generated by the tools in impl/tools and impl/server. No third-party model or motion data is used.
